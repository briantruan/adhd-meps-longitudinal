# Changes in ADHD Medication Utilization Among U.S. Adults Following COVID-19 Telehealth Expansion: A Population-Based Analysis

## Abstract
### Background
Attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) affects approximately 11% of U.S. children and 6% of U.S. adults and is associated with substantial healthcare and economic burden. The COVID-19 pandemic introduced major shifts in care delivery, including expanded telehealth prescribing, yet population-level data on whether these changes affected ADHD medication utilization patterns are limited.

### Methods
We conducted a longitudinal time-series analysis using nationally representative Medical Expenditure Panel Survey (MEPS) data from 2017 through 2023. The study sample included non-Medicare beneficiaries aged ≤65 years with a documented ADHD diagnosis (unweighted N = 4,730). We used the `survey` package to look at trends in ADHD diagnosis, medication expenditures, office-based visits, and telehealth visits post-2020.
